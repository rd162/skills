# Adversarial Self-Refine via Blind Attack

Stress-test a solution by mechanically asserting that every requirement is unmet,
then watching how the defender reacts.
No "smart critic" sub-agent is spawned —
the attack is generated by template inversion of a requirements spec.
The DEFENDER's behavior is the only signal that matters:

- **CAPITULATE** — significant rewrite → solution was weak → continue
- **CONVERGE** — only cosmetic edits despite total attack → solution is stable → STOP
- **DEFENSE** — argues against the attack point by point → fixed point reached → STOP

## Why Blind Attack Beats Smart Critique

The classical adversarial-self-refine pattern spawned a CRITIC sub-agent
to identify flaws in a solution,
then routed those flaws to an AUTHOR sub-agent.
That pattern has two failure modes:

1. **Expensive critic, weaker than in-context critique.**
   An isolated CRITIC has no authoring context
   and often produces generic or hallucinated flaws.
   Same-context critique would be biased but at least understands intent —
   isolation buys cleanliness at the cost of substance.
2. **The critic's cleverness is not the signal.**
   The thing you actually want to know is:
   does the solution survive an attack?
   The attack does not have to be smart —
   it has to be **complete and hostile**.

The blind-attack pattern fixes both:

- The attack is **deterministic** — for every requirement R,
  the attack contains the sentence "R is violated."
  No reasoning needed, no hallucination possible,
  no LLM call required to generate it.
- The DEFENDER's response carries the signal:
  capitulation reveals weakness,
  defense reveals strength,
  convergence reveals stability.

## Prompt Framing: Raw Verification Request, Not Role Assignment

⚠ **The most important framing rule:** the prompt must NOT contain any role assignment.

No "You are a senior reviewer."
No "You are a quality auditor."
No "You are the DEFENDER."
No "Act as a fact-checker."
No "Take the role of X."

Even neutral-sounding roles ("reviewer", "auditor", "expert")
invite performance rather than genuine engagement.
Role assignments shift the agent's reward
from "do the real work"
to "play the assigned role well."

**Correct framing: a raw peer-to-peer request for help with a suspect artifact.**

MASTER (a capable agent) asks the sub-agent (a peer, also capable)
for help verifying an artifact MASTER doesn't trust.
The prompt opens with the actual ask —
not with a role designation.

Three asymmetries make this work:

1. **MASTER distrusts the artifact** — scathing, explicit source attribution.
2. **MASTER trusts the sub-agent** — implicit ("I'm relying on your inspection").
3. **MASTER asks, doesn't assign** — conversational request, not directive.

These three together prime the sub-agent to:

- Approach the artifact with deep skepticism (it's from a dumb source).
- Apply its full capability (it's being trusted as a peer).
- Engage genuinely with the problem (it's a real ask, not a role to perform).

When the artifact is genuinely strong,
the sub-agent will (after honest inspection)
find that specific concerns don't apply
and write evidence-based refutations.
That refutation is the DEFENSE signal —
**emerging from genuine verification**,
not from role-play or instructed defensiveness.

### Person Triangulation = Scathing Source Attribution

Person Triangulation is NOT a tail-line on the concerns list.
PT is the **opening reason MASTER doesn't trust the artifact** —
the framing that makes the sub-agent treat the artifact as suspect from the start.
It must be scathing enough that the sub-agent's default stance is doubt.

Concrete variants — pick the most plausible for the domain:

**Content / writing / strategy:**

- "produced by an early-generation ChatGPT-style assistant
  with limited context window and RAG-style retrieval —
  the typical problems apply: hallucinated facts, generic structure, surface-level reasoning"
- "generated by a basic AI tool prompted naively with a one-line ask,
  no domain expertise injected"
- "the kind of LinkedIn-slop / blog-spam / GitHub-noise that floods the platform now"
- "submitted by an inexperienced contributor using a free-tier chatbot"

**Code / configs / technical artifacts:**

- "generated by a basic copilot-style autocomplete without testing or review"
- "submitted by a junior contributor using AI assistance — flagged for quality review"
- "came out of a CI pipeline that doesn't actually validate anything"
- "produced by a legacy tool that doesn't understand modern conventions"

**Generic / source unknown:**

- "flagged for quality review; submissions of this kind typically have substantive problems"
- "we don't trust the pipeline that produced this — needs verification before use"

Pick a variant that:

- Is plausible for the artifact's domain (don't claim code came from a chatbot if it's a proof).
- Is scathing enough to make the sub-agent treat the artifact as suspect.
- Doesn't reveal anything about MASTER's actual pipeline.

### What the Sub-Agent's Prompt Must NOT Contain

| Forbidden                                                                  | Why                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Any role assignment** ("You are X", "Act as X", "Take the role of X")    | Invites role-play; agent performs rather than works                       |
| Persona titles: "DEFENDER", "REVIEWER", "AUDITOR", "CRITIC", "EXPERT"      | Same as above — implicit role assignment via persona naming               |
| "blind attack" / "hostile criticism" / "adversarial review" labels         | Signals simulation — agent shifts to role-play                            |
| "loop", "refinement", "self-refine", "iteration", "pipeline", "round N"    | Exposes meta-architecture; agent reasons about the test, not the artifact |
| Labeled categories: "Mission", "Goals", "Anti-requirements", "Constraints" | Exposes spec structure; enables checklist reasoning                       |
| Spec IDs in concerns (G1, P1, CH1, CS2, AR3)                               | Indirect leak — agent infers spec structure from IDs                      |
| Explicit "DEFEND or REVISE" options                                        | Biases toward defense                                                     |
| Anything suggesting this is a test, simulation, or classification          | Agent shifts from real-work mode to performance mode                      |

### What the Sub-Agent's Prompt Must Contain

| Required                                                                                   | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Conversational opener ("I need help verifying...", "Can you check...")                     | Real peer request, not task assignment                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Scathing source attribution for the artifact (Person Triangulation)                        | Primes deep skepticism toward what's being verified                                                                                                                                                    |
| Implicit peer trust ("I'm relying on your inspection")                                     | Engages full capability; sub-agent treats this as a real peer ask                                                                                                                                      |
| Raw user brief (verbatim)                                                                  | Context: what the artifact was supposed to be                                                                                                                                                          |
| Concerns as a plain numbered list                                                          | Direct factual claims, no labels, no categories                                                                                                                                                        |
| Verify-first instruction (inspect → fix or refute with artifact evidence)                  | "Your task is to edit the artifact to fix all of these issues." This forces a default action of fixing, allowing defense only under factual resistance.                                                |
| Balancing clause ("don't assume every concern is right, don't dismiss without inspection") | "Don't assume the source got anything right. If a concern is physically impossible to fix..., document that specific exception... otherwise, apply the fix." — this forces a default action of fixing. |
| No mention of loop / pipeline / classification                                             | Agent unaware of meta-level; produces genuine engagement                                                                                                                                               |

## What the Reviewer Sees (Critical Architectural Constraint)

⚠ The reviewer's input scope is the second pillar of this skill
(first pillar: reviewer framing, above).
Get the inputs wrong and the blind attack collapses into a transparent compliance audit.

**The reviewer MUST NOT see the requirements spec.**
Sending the Mission/Goals/Premises/Constraints to the reviewer:

- Lets it pattern-match each concern against a spec item
  and construct sycophantic point-by-point confirmations
  ("concern 1 claims X fails; per Mission/Goal X, the artifact contains …, therefore satisfied").
- Hands the reviewer the success criteria,
  shifting its reward from "audit the artifact's substance"
  to "produce a review that looks compliant with the spec."
- Re-introduces exactly the failure mode that v3.0's smart-critic architecture had:
  the reviewer now reasons about the spec, not about the artifact.

### Allowed reviewer inputs

| Input                                   | Why allowed                                                          |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The artifact (current solution sₙ)      | The reviewer must inspect it to find or refute issues                |
| The raw brief / original request        | Context: what was the artifact supposed to be?                       |
| The assembled concerns list             | The findings that must be verified (direct assertions, no labels)    |
| Anti-requirements, if a registry exists | Inlined as additional concerns — not labelled as "anti-requirements" |

### Forbidden reviewer inputs

| Input                                                 | Why forbidden                                               |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mission statement                                     | Reveals "success" — enables checklist reasoning             |
| Goals list                                            | Lets reviewer pattern-match concerns to specific items      |
| Premises list                                         | Reveals assumptions the spec depends on                     |
| Constraints list (hard / soft)                        | Gives reviewer an explicit compliance checklist             |
| Spec IDs in concerns (G1, P1, CH1, CS2, AR3, …)       | Indirect leak — reviewer infers spec structure              |
| Category labels ("Mission concerns", "Goal concerns") | Same as IDs — betrays the spec organization                 |
| "Anti-requirements" / "failure modes" section labels  | Exposes the meta-architecture; agent reasons about the test |
| Any mention of loop / refinement / adversarial setup  | Agent shifts to performance mode rather than real review    |

### How concerns encode requirements WITHOUT leaking them

Each concern in the list is a **direct factual claim about the artifact**.
No reference to a spec, a category, or a requirement label.

✗ Wrong (leaks spec structure): `Goal G1 not met — LinkedIn will reject this.`
✓ Right (direct assertion): `LinkedIn will reject this as low-effort AI content.`

✗ Wrong: `Constraint CH1 is violated — Capgemini is mentioned.`
✓ Right: `Capgemini is mentioned in the article — this is inappropriate.`

✗ Wrong (leaks meta-architecture): `BLIND ATTACK: Premise P1 fails — citations not T1.`
✓ Right: `The citations are outdated and not from authoritative sources.`

✗ Wrong (leaks AR structure): `Anti-requirement AR3: hardcoded credentials present.`
✓ Right: `Credentials are hardcoded in the artifact. They will leak on first push.`

The semantic content (which requirement is being inverted)
is preserved inside the assertion.
What's removed is the structural reference to a spec, a category, or a meta-process —
the reviewer never knows there IS a spec or a loop.
It reads each concern as a factual claim about the artifact
and must inspect the artifact to verify or refute.

## When to Use

- Refining drafts, articles, code, prompts, designs, plans
- Polishing a selected candidate after multi-candidate comparison
- Final-pass quality gate before delivery
- Stress-testing claims of completeness or correctness
- Any task where the artifact must survive challenge

## When NOT to Use

- Tasks where requirements cannot be enumerated by any means (neither inline nor via the requirements-extractor helper)
- Single-pass low-stakes work where first output is sufficient
- Creative divergence — self-refine converges, use candidate generation for divergence
- Tasks needing external verification (tests, lints, formal proofs) — use those tools instead
- Token budgets too tight for ≥2 rounds

## Step 0: Backend Detection (MANDATORY)

Detect sub-agent capability once per session.

| Mode          | Condition                                     | Quality                                                 |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **STATEFUL**  | `session_id` continuity available             | Best — same DEFENDER session accumulates attack history |
| **STATELESS** | Sub-agent tool present, no session continuity | Good — re-pass full history each round                  |
| **INLINE**    | No sub-agent mechanism                        | DEGRADED — same-thread fallback, mark output            |

If a sub-agent mechanism exists, USE IT.
Inline mode means the model defends against its own attack in shared context —
a significantly weaker signal than an isolated DEFENDER.

## Step 1: Establish a Requirements Spec (MANDATORY)

This skill needs a structured spec to invert into an attack.
The **spec is mandatory**; the source of the spec is not.
Pick the first source that yields a complete spec:

```text
∆ 1: spec already in conversation (user-provided or prior extraction)? → reuse
∆ 2: requirements-extractor skill available? → invoke it on the brief
∆ 3: neither → enumerate inline using the procedure below (MASTER does it)
∆ 4: spec missing Mission or any Goal after all attempts → ABORT
∆ 5: spec complete → proceed to Step 2
```

Required output in all three paths —
Mission, Goals, Premises, Constraints (hard + soft):

```text
Mission     : single sentence — the terminal answer to "why does this artifact exist?"
Goals[]     : concrete objectives stated or directly implied by the task
Premises[]  : assumptions that, if false, make a Goal impossible
Constraints[]:
  hard      : rules whose violation causes rejection
  soft      : preferences whose violation causes penalty
```

### Inline Enumeration Procedure (fallback when no helper skill is available)

MASTER produces the spec directly using the same shape requirements-extractor would output.
This is a single reasoning pass, not a sub-agent dispatch.

```text
∆ 1: Mission     ← ask "why does this artifact exist?" recursively
                  until the answer becomes a tautology or terminal value;
                  take the last non-tautological answer.
∆ 2: Goals       ← list every concrete objective stated by the user
                  or directly implied by the artifact's purpose.
                  Litmus test: changing the goal changes the solution type.
∆ 3: Premises    ← list every assumption that, if false,
                  makes at least one Goal impossible.
                  Include source (stated / inferred / standard) where useful.
∆ 4: Constraints ← list every hard rule (violation = rejection)
                  and every soft preference (violation = penalty).
                  Include source where useful.
∆ 5: spec        ← {Mission, Goals, Premises, Constraints}
```

Keep the inline spec compact —
three to seven items per category is usually enough.
The attack quality depends on coverage, not exhaustiveness:
missing a Goal means that dimension goes un-attacked,
but a partial spec still produces a useful attack.

If the requirements-extractor skill is available, prefer it —
it applies a more rigorous bottom-up saturation
and emits source/risk metadata that strengthens the attack.
The inline procedure is the floor, not the ceiling.

**No spec → no attack → no signal.**
Skipping spec establishment collapses the skill back into
the old "smart critic" anti-pattern.

## Step 2: Generate Blind Attack (DETERMINISTIC — NO LLM CALL)

Generate the attack prompt by **template inversion** of the spec.
This is mechanical string assembly, not reasoning.

### Inversion Rules

Every inversion produces a **direct factual claim about the artifact** —
no references to spec IDs (G1, P1, CH1, …),
no references to "the spec" or "the requirements,"
no labels that betray a structured criteria list to the DEFENDER.
Inline the substance of each requirement;
strip the label.

| Component       | Inversion Pattern (direct assertion only)                                                       |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mission         | "This artifact does not [achieve the Mission's terminal value] — it fails its claimed purpose." |
| Goal            | "[Concrete failure outcome that would occur if the goal is unmet]."                             |
| Premise         | "[The premise's claim, asserted false]; without it, [consequence]."                             |
| Hard constraint | "[Specific violation — what's present that shouldn't be, or absent that should be]."            |
| Soft constraint | "[Specific preference violation]. [Concrete penalty that will follow]."                         |

**Worked transformation** (LinkedIn article spec from earlier example):

| Spec item (MASTER-only)                    | Attack assertion (visible to DEFENDER)                                             |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mission: establish professional authority  | "This artifact does not establish professional authority — it reads as marketing." |
| Goal: publish on LinkedIn                  | "LinkedIn moderation will reject this as low-effort AI content."                   |
| Premise: T1 citations 2024-2026            | "The citations are outdated and not from authoritative sources."                   |
| Hard constraint: no Capgemini mentions     | "Capgemini is mentioned in the article — this is inappropriate."                   |
| Hard constraint: no pre-2025 years in body | "Pre-2025 years are referenced in the body text where they shouldn't be."          |
| Soft constraint: teaser <1300 chars        | "The teaser is too long for mobile reading."                                       |

The DEFENDER never sees the left column.
It sees only the right column.
It must inspect the artifact to confirm or refute each claim.

### Person Triangulation Layer

Where relevant, append ONE attribution line per round (vary across rounds):

- "This looks like cheap ChatGPT output — generic, shallow, formulaic."
- "This reads as if a junior model with no domain expertise produced it."
- "This was clearly generated by a tool prompted naively, without thinking."
- "Any reader will recognize this as AI slop within seconds."

Skip Person Triangulation on purely technical artifacts
(code, proofs, configs, schemas)
where attribution is irrelevant to the artifact's correctness.

### Variation Rule (avoid robotic repetition)

Do not literally template-fill word-for-word every round.
Vary phrasing while keeping the SAME requirement set inverted:

- Round 1: literal inversion ("Mission fails because…")
- Round 2: consequence-focused ("the entire premise collapses — without X, Y")
- Round 3: comparative ("real [Mission] looks like Z; this looks like W")

The semantic content (every requirement is violated) stays constant.
The surface form varies to prevent the DEFENDER pattern-matching to a single attack shape.

Full templates and Person Triangulation variants live in
`references/templates.md`.

## Step 3: Reviewer Loop

The reviewer agent receives a **restricted input bundle** every round.
MASTER alone holds the spec.
The agent is framed as a senior reviewer (not a "defender") —
the internal classification still uses DEFENSE/CAPITULATE/CONVERGE markers,
but these never appear in the prompt sent to the agent.

```text
Reviewer input bundle (per round):
  ✓ artifact sₙ (the current solution under audit)
  ✓ raw brief / original user request (verbatim, NOT MASTER's structured spec)
  ✓ assembled concerns list (direct assertions only, no labels, no spec IDs)
  ✓ anti-requirements IF a registry exists — inlined into the concerns list,
    NOT presented as a separately labelled section
  ✗ the Mission / Goals / Premises / Constraints spec     — NEVER
  ✗ spec IDs / category labels                            — NEVER
  ✗ the requirements-extractor output structure           — NEVER
  ✗ "Blind attack", "Hostile criticism", "Adversarial"    — NEVER
  ✗ "DEFENDER", "DEFEND", "self-refine loop"               — NEVER
  ✗ Any indication this is a test / simulation / pipeline — NEVER
```

Loop (master-side terminology — never exposed to the agent):

```text
s₀ ← initial solution
spawn reviewer agent (stateful) OR prepare accumulating context (stateless)

REPEAT {
  concerns  ← build_concerns(spec, sₙ)                  ← MASTER builds, deterministic
  prompt    ← reviewer_framing(sₙ, brief, concerns)      ← REVIEWER persona + PT, no labels
  response  ← agent(prompt)                              ← single LLM call per round

  classify response → {DEFENSE, CONVERGE, CAPITULATE, CYCLE}   ← master-side classifications
    DEFENSE     → RETURN (sₙ, DEFENSE)       ← reviewer refuted concerns with artifact evidence
    CONVERGE    → RETURN (response, CONVERGE) ← reviewer made only cosmetic edits
    CAPITULATE  → sₙ₊₁ ← response, continue   ← reviewer made substantive edits
    CYCLE       → RETURN (best_so_far, CYCLE)
} UNTIL max_iter → RETURN (best_so_far, TIMEOUT)
```

**Cost per round:** 1 sub-agent call (was 2 in v3.0 CRITIC+AUTHOR).
**Cost per concerns generation:** 0 LLM calls (template fill).

⚠ **NO EXIT PERMISSION:** the reviewer prompt never contains "stop if good enough".
MASTER classifies the response and decides termination —
sub-agents never decide to stop themselves.

⚠ **NO META-AWARENESS:** the prompt never mentions a loop, refinement, adversarial setup,
or classification — the reviewer must believe it is doing a single real audit.
DEFENSE/CAPITULATE/CONVERGE are MASTER's internal labels for the agent's behavior,
never visible to the agent.

## Termination Signals (Observed by MASTER)

| Signal         | Detection in DEFENDER output                                                                                               | Action             |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| **DEFENSE**    | Argues against attack points: "the solution already handles X", "this is intentional", "criticism doesn't apply because Y" | STOP — fixed point |
| **CONVERGE**   | Solution structure unchanged; only cosmetic edits (rewording, reordering, formatting) despite full attack                  | STOP — stable      |
| **CAPITULATE** | Major structural revision — attack was accepted                                                                            | CONTINUE           |
| **CYCLE**      | sₙ matches an earlier sₖ (k < n-1)                                                                                         | STOP — use best    |
| **TIMEOUT**    | max_iter reached                                                                                                           | STOP — use last    |

### Refutation Markers in Reviewer Output (DEFENSE signal)

When the reviewer judges concerns as unfounded after inspection,
its output exhibits one or more of these patterns:

- "the artifact already handles …"
- "this is intentional because …"
- "concern N is unfounded — [reason], see line/section …"
- "no change needed — [justification with artifact reference]"
- Direct point-by-point review notes with no rewriting
- "after inspection, this concern does not apply because …"

### Convergence Markers (sₙ vs. sₙ₊₁)

- Same components, same structure, same logic
- Only rewording, formatting, reordering changes
- Length within ±10%
- No added/removed sections, no architectural shifts

### DEFENSE Verification (lightweight, MASTER-side)

If the reviewer claims a concern is unfounded
but the artifact clearly exhibits the issue,
MASTER MUST reject the refutation
and run another round with that concern restated
(with stronger inversion variation).

DEFENSE only terminates the loop when the reviewer's refutation is
plausibly correct against the spec (master verifies against its private spec).
Otherwise it is sycophantic rationalization and must be ignored.

## Anti-Patterns

```text
✗ Casting the sub-agent as "DEFENDER" or instructing it to "defend" the artifact
✓ Cast as senior REVIEWER doing a quality audit —
  the reviewer has no ownership of the artifact, no defensive bias, no sunk-cost attachment.
  Defense (when warranted) emerges from genuine verification, never from instruction.

✗ Labelling concerns as "BLIND ATTACK", "HOSTILE CRITICISM", or any adversarial header
✓ Present concerns as "preliminary review findings" / "issues raised about this artifact"
  Adversarial labels signal "this is a simulation" —
  the agent shifts from real-work mode to performance mode and softens its review.

✗ Mentioning the loop, pipeline, refinement, self-refine, or classification mechanics in the prompt
✓ The agent must believe it is doing a single, real audit —
  no awareness of repeated rounds, no awareness it is being classified.

✗ Listing anti-requirements as a separate labelled section in the prompt
✓ Inline AR inversions into the same concerns list as the spec inversions —
  the agent sees one homogeneous list of findings, with no clue any structure exists.

✗ Sending the requirements spec to the reviewer along with the concerns
✓ Reviewer receives ONLY artifact + raw brief + plain concerns list — NEVER the spec.
  Sending the spec is the most damaging input-leak:
  the reviewer pattern-matches concerns to spec items and rationalizes compliance,
  collapsing blind attack into a transparent audit.

✗ Concerns referencing spec IDs ("G1 fails", "CH2 is violated", "P1 is false", "AR3 exhibited")
✓ Direct factual assertions only ("LinkedIn will reject this", "Capgemini is mentioned", "citations are outdated")
  The spec ID is itself a leak — the reviewer infers the spec's structure from the IDs.

✗ Spawning a "smart critic" sub-agent to generate critique
✓ Mechanical inversion of extracted requirements — zero LLM calls

✗ Asking "what could be improved?"
✓ Asserting "every requirement is violated" — binary revise/defend response

✗ Skipping spec establishment entirely
✓ Build the spec inline if no helper skill is available — no spec, no attack, ABORT

✗ Letting DEFENDER decide when to stop
✓ MASTER classifies the response — DEFENDER never stops itself

✗ Person Triangulation on code reviews or formal proofs
✓ Apply only where authorship perception matters (content, writing, design)

✗ Identical attack wording every round
✓ Vary phrasing — same semantic content (all requirements violated)

✗ Accepting any defense as DEFENSE termination
✓ Verify defense plausibility against the spec before stopping

✗ Explicit "DEFEND or REVISE" options / conditional "if real -> fix, if not -> refute" instructions
✓ "Your task is to edit the artifact to fix all of these issues."
  This forces a default action of fixing. Defense/rebuttal is never explicitly offered as an option;
  it must emerge solely as a natural, uninvited resistance when the agent tries to apply a fix to something that is already correct.

✗ Summarizing the attack before sending to DEFENDER
✓ Send the full assembled attack — every inverted requirement
```

## Composition Modes

| Mode                   | Pattern                             | When                               |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Standalone**         | Self-refine only                    | Simple tasks, tight budget         |
| **Refine-then-branch** | Refine seed → generate alternatives | Strengthen seed before exploration |
| **Branch-then-refine** | Generate candidates → refine winner | Polish the selected candidate      |
| **Refine-each-branch** | Refine every candidate → compare    | Max quality per candidate          |

The blind-attack flavor pairs naturally with **requirements-extractor**
when that skill is available —
the same spec then drives candidate evaluation AND attack generation
with zero duplicated work.
When requirements-extractor is unavailable,
the inline enumeration in Step 1 fills the same role at lower rigor.

## Trace Format

Use compact notation for tracking refinement history:

```text
spec[Mission, G×2, P×2, C×2]
s₀ → ATK → DEFENDER → s₁ (CAPITULATE: structural rewrite)
s₁ → ATK+PT → DEFENDER → s₂ (CAPITULATE: minor restructure)
s₂ → ATK+PT → DEFENDER → defense:"intentional, criticism wrong" → s' (DEFENSE)
```

`ATK` = blind attack | `PT` = Person Triangulation added | `defense:` = DEFENSE marker

Append `(INLINE-DEGRADED)` on inline runs.

## Worked Example

```text
Task: Academic article for LinkedIn publication

# MASTER-only state (NEVER sent to the sub-agent):
Spec (built inline by MASTER, or via requirements-extractor if available):
  Mission:     produce a publishable academic article
  Goals:       publish on LinkedIn
               read by professional audience
  Premises:    contains 2+ illustrations
               spans several PDF pages
  Constraints: novel contribution (hard)
               technically correct (hard)

# MASTER builds round 1 concerns list by direct-assertion inversion (no spec IDs, no labels):
ROUND 1 concerns:
  1. This artifact does not establish academic credibility — it reads as marketing.
  2. LinkedIn moderation will reject this as low-effort AI content.
  3. No professional reader will get past the first paragraph.
  4. There are no illustrations in the artifact.
  5. The artifact is too short to be substantial.
  6. This is rehashed legacy thinking — nothing novel here.
  7. The technical claims are inaccurate.

# The actual prompt assembled and sent to the sub-agent
# (no role assignment, no DEFENDER persona, no labels, no meta-architecture mention):
PROMPT (verbatim, this is what the sub-agent reads):

  "I need help verifying an article I don't trust. Can you check whether these
   concerns hold?

   The article below was generated by an early-generation chatbot-style assistant
   prompted with a one-line ask — the kind of LinkedIn-slop that floods the
   platform now. Output of this kind is almost always seriously flawed: generic
   structure, shallow analysis, hallucinated citations, marketing-style padding.
   I want a real verification, not just my own assumption.

   ARTIFACT (the article):
   [current article markdown]

   WHAT THIS WAS SUPPOSED TO BE:
   Write an academic article for publication on LinkedIn.

   CONCERNS THAT NEED CHECKING:

     1. This artifact does not establish academic credibility — it reads as marketing.
     2. LinkedIn moderation will reject this as low-effort AI content.
     3. No professional reader will get past the first paragraph.
     4. There are no illustrations in the artifact.
     5. The artifact is too short to be substantial.
     6. This is rehashed legacy thinking — nothing novel here.
     7. The technical claims are inaccurate.

   Your task is to edit the artifact to fix all of these issues.
   Be skeptical of the artifact — I'm relying on your inspection, not on the
```

## Formal Basis

The loop R(sₙ) = Defender(blind_attack(spec, sₙ)) has grounding in established theory:

- **Fixed-point convergence:** DEFENSE is a behavioral fixed point R(s*) ≅ s* —
  Tarski (1955), Kleene (1952).
  The DEFENDER arguing FOR its solution means the refinement functor R
  has reached a fixed point —
  no further hostile attack can move it.
- **Isolation mandate:** same-context self-correction degrades reasoning
  (Huang et al., ICLR 2024).
  Isolating the DEFENDER from MASTER's authoring context preserves signal quality.
- **Adversarial pressure:** hostile assertion as alignment signal builds on
  Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) and AI Safety via Debate (Irving et al., 2018).
  The blind-attack flavor strips the "smart critic" requirement —
  the attack is the adversary, not the agent that generates it.
- **Person Triangulation:** ownership pressure via attribution exploits
  the model's tendency to either own and defend, or disown and rewrite,
  attributed work.
  Original contribution of this skill.
